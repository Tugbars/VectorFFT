1The tiling

An N1 × N2 × N3 cube runs axis 0 over the whole cube, the 2D column pass at a pitch of N2·N3, then a structure on every N2 × N3 plane: a 2D child plan, or this tier's own axis-1 pass and rows, the flat structure. One plan is one point of the product

E = L × C × A × S × F × T,
(1)

where L is the cache level of the cube (4), C the class of the axis-0 length N1 (3), A the axis-0 machinery (5), S the plane structure (2), F the threaded form (6) and T the thread count. In Figure 1 a tile is one class of N1 at one cache level, a box one piece the tier races there, its italic line the threaded forms.

3The other sheets

Each 3D kind has a sheet of its own, built from the 2D and 1D sheets' boxes.

  • 3D real (R2C, C2R). The walks are fixed: R2C runs the planes first, C2R axis 0 first. The structures raced are the 2D real child per plane; pay-once, where axis 0 lands planes and row blocks at their natural places in one write; and a band arm for C2R. Axis 0 runs in place, by dense column strips, or stage by stage (threaded only, R2C). Out of place, natural order.
  • Lane-major batches. K cubes side by side: axis 0 over every lane, then a 2D lane-major child for the planes; a real batch inherits the child's row groups.
  • Contiguous batches. The volume queue: K walks of the single-cube plan, one a cube.
  • Each CPU, ISA and precision. L1d sets the one-thread bound; L2 against the plane's size decides when the half team pays; FP32 halves the bytes, so a cube can sit a cache level lower.

Note. Axis 0, and the flat structure's axis 1, are the 2D column pass at a wider pitch: the column machinery of the 2D sheet applies to them. The 2D child's planes are cells of the 2D sheet, and the rows are cells of the 1D sheet. A change on either sheet reaches these tiles.
